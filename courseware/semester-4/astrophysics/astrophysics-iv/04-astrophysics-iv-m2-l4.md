# The Expanding Universe and the Hubble–Lemaître Law

## Overview

In 1929 the Universe had a revolution: distant galaxies are receding, faster the farther they are — space itself is expanding. That discovery, built on Slipher's redshifts and Hubble's Cepheid distances, replaced the static Universe with a dynamic one and turned cosmology into a measurement science. This lesson covers the phenomenon (redshift as stretching of space), the law (v = H₀d), and the number that matters most in cosmology — the Hubble constant H₀ — whose measurement today is precise enough to expose a genuine crisis: different first-rate methods disagree at high significance. Measuring H₀ is also a masterclass in experimental method: the distance ladder, a chain of calibrations from Solar System parallaxes to supernovae in galaxies hundreds of millions of light-years away.

## Learning Path

1. Meet the data: galaxy redshifts grow with distance.
2. Interpret redshift correctly — expansion of space, not motion through space.
3. State the Hubble–Lemaître law and define H₀, the Hubble time, and the Hubble distance.
4. Build the distance ladder rung by rung and trace the error budget.
5. Confront the Hubble tension between the local ladder and the early-Universe inference.
6. See how expansion implies a hot, dense past — the setup for next lesson.

## Core Explanation

### The observation: redshifts with distance

Vesto Slipher (1912–1925) measured the spectra of spiral "nebulae" and found most shifted toward the red — velocities up to ~1000 km/s, far beyond any star. Hubble and Humason added distances (from Cepheid variables) and found the correlation: recession velocity proportional to distance,

v = H₀ d

with proportionality constant H₀ — the Hubble constant (its name credits Lemaître's earlier theoretical and empirical work). Modern values cluster around H₀ ≈ 70 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹: a galaxy 100 Mpc away recedes at ~7000 km/s.

### What is actually expanding

The redshift is not a Doppler shift of galaxies flying through static space. In general relativity the metric itself evolves: light emitted at wavelength λ_e stretches with the scale factor a(t) of the Universe:

1 + z = λ_obs/λ_e = a(t_obs)/a(t_e)

For small z this reduces to v ≈ cz and the linear law; for distant objects the full relativistic relation must be used. Two important consequences:

1. **No centre, no edge.** Every observer sees the same law — the raisin-bread analogy: every raisin sees the others receding with speed proportional to distance.
2. **Superluminal recession is allowed.** Beyond the Hubble distance d_H = c/H₀ ≈ 4300 Mpc (for H₀ = 70), recession exceeds c — not motion through space, but metric expansion, so no relativity violation.

### The numbers that H₀ sets

H₀ is not just a proportionality constant; it sets the scales of cosmology:

- **Hubble time:** t_H = 1/H₀ ≈ 14 billion years (for H₀ = 70). For constant expansion this equals the age; in real models the age differs by a factor set by the contents of the Universe (next lessons).
- **Hubble distance:** d_H = c/H₀ ≈ 4.3 Gpc — the scale at which expansion becomes relativistic.

Measuring H₀ therefore simultaneously measures the Universe's size and age scales — which is why it has been pursued for a century.

### The distance ladder

H₀ = v/d needs both redshifts (easy: spectra) and distances (hard: no cosmic tape measure). The ladder builds distances from overlapping calibrations:

1. **Parallax** (Gaia): geometry gives distances to ~10 kpc directly — the anchor rung.
2. **Cepheid variables:** period-luminosity relation (Leavitt law) calibrated by parallax; reaches ~30 Mpc.
3. **Type Ia supernovae:** standardisable candles (Astrophysics III) calibrated by Cepheids in host galaxies; reach hundreds of Mpc into the smooth Hubble flow.

Each rung transfers the calibration upward; the error budget compounds. The local-ladder result today: H₀ ≈ 73 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ (SH0ES). A completely independent route infers H₀ from the cosmic microwave background (next lesson) under the ΛCDM model: H₀ ≈ 67.4 (Planck). The ~5σ gap is the **Hubble tension** — either an unrecognised systematic in one ladder, or new physics in the standard model. It is one of the field's central open problems.

### Expansion implies a hot past

Running the film backwards: at earlier times a(t) was smaller, densities and temperatures higher. Extrapolation reaches a hot, dense state ~13.8 billion years ago — the Big Bang. Expansion thus makes two predictions: a relic radiation field (the CMB, next lesson) and a fossil nuclear record (primordial nucleosynthesis). Both were confirmed, and cosmology became physical science rather than speculation.

## Key Ideas

- Distant galaxies' redshifts grow with distance: v = H₀d, discovered by Slipher, Hubble, and Lemaître.
- Cosmological redshift is metric expansion: 1 + z = a(t_obs)/a(t_e); galaxies do not fly through space, space stretches between them.
- H₀ ≈ 70 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ sets the Hubble time (~14 Gyr) and Hubble distance (~4.3 Gpc).
- The distance ladder chains parallax → Cepheids → Type Ia supernovae; each rung calibrates the next.
- The Hubble tension: local ladder (~73) versus CMB inference (~67.4) disagree at ~5σ — systematics or new physics.
- Backward extrapolation implies a hot dense early Universe, predicting the CMB and primordial element abundances.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Recession velocity.**
A galaxy at 250 Mpc with H₀ = 70: v = 70 × 250 = 17 500 km/s ≈ 0.058c. At this speed the non-relativistic form is adequate (z ≈ 0.058).

**Example 2 — Distance from redshift.**
A quasar spectrum shows z = 0.15 (Hubble flow dominated): v ≈ cz = 45 000 km/s → d = v/H₀ = 45 000/70 ≈ 640 Mpc. For z ≳ 0.3 the full relativistic distance relation replaces this estimate.

**Example 3 — Hubble time.**
t_H = 1/H₀ = 1 Mpc / 70 km s⁻¹ = 3.086 × 10¹⁹ km / 70 km s⁻¹ ≈ 4.4 × 10¹⁷ s ≈ 14.0 Gyr. The measured age of 13.8 Gyr differs by ~1.5% — expansion has been close to constant-velocity on average, a remarkable coincidence with deep content-physics reasons (dark energy).

**Example 4 — Ladder propagation.**
If the Cepheid zero-point calibration is off by 2%, every supernova luminosity inherits it, and H₀ shifts by ~2% — larger than current statistical errors. This is why the Gaia parallax anchor receives such scrutiny, and why independent ladders (tip of the red giant branch, surface brightness fluctuations, strong-lensing time delays) matter.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Galaxies recede because the Big Bang was an explosion into space."** Expansion is space itself growing; there is no centre, no edge, no into.
2. **"Recession faster than light violates relativity."** Special relativity bounds motion *through* space; metric expansion has no such limit, and we observe galaxies whose recession exceeds c.
3. **"Redshift always means velocity."** At low z, cz is a good proxy; at cosmological z the interpretation is stretched wavelengths, and peculiar velocities add noise at short distances.
4. **"The Hubble tension is a measurement error that will go away."** It may be, but both sides have been stress-tested for years; it is treated as a potential discovery, not a nuisance.
5. **"H₀ measures how fast the Universe expands now, once and for all."** It is the expansion *rate today*; the rate changes over cosmic history — dark energy makes it history-dependent (lesson 6).

## Quick Check

1. Why is cosmological redshift not an ordinary Doppler shift?
2. Compute the recession velocity of a cluster at 800 Mpc (H₀ = 70), and comment on the approximation used.
3. Name the three main rungs of the distance ladder and what transfers calibration between them.
4. What are the two values at odds in the Hubble tension, and how is each obtained?
5. What two relics does backward extrapolation of expansion predict?

**Answers:**
1. It is the stretching of photon wavelengths with the expanding scale factor, 1 + z = a_now/a_then — not motion through a fixed background; every observer sees the same pattern.
2. v = 70 × 800 = 56 000 km/s ≈ 0.19c; v = cz is still a fair approximation at z ≈ 0.19, though relativistic corrections are starting to matter.
3. Parallax (geometry) → Cepheids (period-luminosity) → Type Ia supernovae (standardisable candles); Cepheids are anchored by parallaxes and supernovae are anchored by Cepheids in host galaxies.
4. Local ladder H₀ ≈ 73 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ versus CMB/ΛCDM inference ≈ 67.4 — a ~5σ gap between fully independent methods.
5. The cosmic microwave background (relic radiation of the hot phase) and the primordial light-element abundances from Big Bang nucleosynthesis.

## Takeaway

One straight line — velocity against distance — replaced the eternal static Universe with an evolving one and gave cosmology its master parameter. Measuring H₀ chains every distance technique the field possesses, and its current disagreement between methods may be the first crack in the standard model found from pure measurement. Expansion also points backward: a hotter, denser past whose relics we can still detect. Next lesson goes there.
