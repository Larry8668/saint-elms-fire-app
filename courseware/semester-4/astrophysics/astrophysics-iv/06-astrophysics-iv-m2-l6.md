# Dark Matter, Dark Energy and the ΛCDM Model

## Overview

The inventory from the CMB reads like a confession of ignorance: ordinary matter is ~5% of the cosmic budget. The rest is two dark components with opposite gravitational personalities. Dark matter clumps — it built the scaffolding on which galaxies formed, and its evidence spans rotation curves, cluster dynamics, gravitational lenses, and colliding clusters. Dark energy does not clump at all — it permeates space uniformly and drives the expansion to accelerate, revealed by Type Ia supernovae in 1998 and now central to every cosmological measurement. This lesson presents both: the evidence chain for dark matter, the discovery and physics of acceleration, and the ΛCDM model — six parameters that fit everything from nucleosynthesis to the CMB to baryon acoustic oscillations, yet leave the nature of 95% of the Universe unknown.

## Core Explanation

### Dark matter: the evidence chain

**Galaxies.** Flat rotation curves (Module 1) imply M(< r) ∝ r far beyond the luminous disc: every spiral sits inside a dark halo of ~10× its visible mass. Dwarf spheroidal galaxies — almost no stars, huge velocity dispersions — are the most dark-matter-dominated objects known.

**Clusters.** Fritz Zwicky (1933) applied the virial theorem to the Coma cluster: galaxy velocities imply ~400× more mass than the luminous galaxies provide (modern values, with better distances and including gas: ~5–10×). Hot X-ray gas in clusters, weighing in via its temperature and hydrostatic balance, confirms the deficit independently.

**Gravitational lensing.** Mass bends light (general relativity); the distortion pattern maps the lensing mass regardless of whether it shines. Weak-lensing surveys reconstruct dark halo shapes; strong lenses (arcs, Einstein rings) weigh cluster cores. Lensing maps agree with dynamics: the mass is where the stars are not.

**The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-56).** Two clusters collided: the hot gas (most of the ordinary matter) shock-braked and lagged at the centre, while the galaxies and the lensing-inferred mass sailed through. The mass is offset from the dominant baryonic component — direct evidence that the missing mass is a collisionless substance, not modified gravity acting on the visible matter alone.

**CMB and structure.** The acoustic peaks require ~27% non-baryonic matter: dark matter's gravity seeds structure before recombination (unlike baryons, which are held back by photon pressure), and the observed web of galaxies matches simulations only with cold (slow-moving) dark matter.

### What is it?

Candidates: WIMPs (weak-scale particles, the historic favourite — direct-detection experiments have so far found nothing), axions (light, wave-like; active searches), sterile neutrinos, and primordial black holes (constrained to limited mass windows). Neutrinos are real dark matter but too hot and light to be the main component. The search spans particle colliders, deep-underground detectors, and astrophysical indirect signatures (annihilation gamma rays). Nature has not yet chosen.

### Accelerating expansion

Type Ia supernovae (standardisable candles, Astrophysics III) let two teams (1998) measure the expansion history. Distant supernovae (z ≈ 0.5) appeared ~25% fainter than expected for a decelerating Universe: the expansion has been **speeding up**. Nobel 2011.

Dynamics: in general relativity, a homogeneous component with density ρ and pressure p gravitates with effective source ρ + 3p/c². Ordinary matter (p ≈ 0) decelerates; radiation more so. A component with sufficiently negative pressure — equation of state w = p/(ρc²) < −1/3 — accelerates. The cosmological constant Λ has exactly w = −1: constant density as space expands, repulsive gravity. Observed supernova distances, the CMB, and baryon acoustic oscillations all converge on w ≈ −1.

The **deceleration parameter** q = −äa/ȧ² flips sign: q > 0 during matter domination, q < 0 since z ≈ 0.7 when dark energy took over. The Universe braked for nine billion years, then hit the accelerator.

### The vacuum-energy puzzle

Λ corresponds to an energy density ~6 × 10⁻¹⁰ J/m³ — tiny cosmologically, monstrous theoretically. Naive quantum-field estimates of vacuum energy exceed it by ~10¹²⁰. Whether Λ is truly constant or a slowly evolving field (quintessence) is being tested by next-generation surveys; any evolution would rewrite the model.

### The ΛCDM model

Lambda-CDM: general relativity plus a cosmological constant (Λ) and cold dark matter (CDM), with six parameters (baryon density, dark-matter density, acoustic scale, amplitude and tilt of primordial fluctuations, reionisation optical depth). Its successes: BBN abundances, CMB spectrum and peaks, large-scale structure and baryon acoustic oscillations, supernova distances, and lensing — a single consistent model across 13.8 billion years.

Its problems: the nature of both dark components; the Hubble tension (lesson 4) and a milder S₈ (structure-growth) tension; small-scale puzzles in dwarf galaxies (core–cusp, missing satellites) that may be baryonic feedback or a crack in CDM. ΛCDM is the standard model of cosmology — not because it is complete, but because nothing fits better, and because its failures are now measurable.

## Key Ideas

- Dark matter evidence is multi-scale: rotation curves, virial cluster masses, lensing maps, the Bullet Cluster offset, and CMB/structure requirements.
- Dark matter must be non-baryonic, cold, and collisionless; its particle identity is unknown (WIMPs, axions, sterile neutrinos, PBHs all unconfirmed).
- Type Ia supernovae revealed accelerating expansion (1998); a component with w < −1/3 drives it; Λ (w = −1) fits.
- Dark energy is ~68% of the budget, uniform, and of unknown origin; its tiny density versus huge quantum estimates is the vacuum-energy problem.
- ΛCDM's six parameters fit nucleosynthesis through the CMB to the present-day web — with tensions (H₀, S₈) marking the frontier.
- Roughly 95% of the cosmic inventory is dark; known matter is the trace contaminant.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Virial mass of a cluster.**
Coma-type cluster: velocity dispersion σ ≈ 1000 km/s, radius R ≈ 2 Mpc. Virial estimate M ≈ 5Rσ²/G:

M ≈ 5 × 6.2 × 10²² × 10¹² / 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ ≈ 4.6 × 10⁴⁵ kg ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁵ M_☉

Visible galaxies hold ~10¹⁴ M_☉ — a factor-of-10+ discrepancy, Zwicky's argument in one line.

**Example 2 — Lensing mass.**
A strong lens produces an Einstein ring of radius θ_E. The enclosed mass scales as M ∝ θ_E² D_lens D_source/D_relative; a cluster arc with θ_E = 30″ at cosmological distances encloses ~10¹³–10¹⁴ M_☉ inside that ring — model-free, and matching the dark-halo picture.

**Example 3 — The supernova gap.**
A z = 0.5 Type Ia with expected m = 22.7 in a decelerating model is observed at m = 23.2: Δm = 0.5 mag means fainter by 10^(0.2) ≈ 1.58 — hence ~25–30% farther. Fainter at fixed redshift = accelerated expansion in between.

**Example 4 — Dark-energy takeover.**
Matter dilutes as a⁻³, Λ stays constant: equality when ρ_m(a) = ρ_Λ, i.e. a_eq = (Ω_m/Ω_Λ)^{1/3} = (0.3/0.7)^{1/3} ≈ 0.75 → z ≈ 0.33 for equality, with dynamical takeover (q = 0) at z ≈ 0.7. Seven billion years ago, the Universe switched regimes.

## Common Misconceptions

1. **"Dark matter is just unseen ordinary matter."** BBN and the CMB fix the baryon budget at ~5%; dark matter must be a different substance, and dust/gas/dwarfs are observationally insufficient.
2. **"Modified gravity explains everything without dark matter."** It addresses rotation curves but struggles with the Bullet Cluster offset, the CMB peaks, and structure growth simultaneously.
3. **"Acceleration means galaxies fly apart."** Bound systems (galaxies, clusters, solar systems) do not participate in the acceleration; only the largest unbound scales feel it.
4. **"Dark energy is antigravity."** It is gravity — with negative pressure, which in general relativity gravitates repulsively. No new force required; the source is unusual.
5. **"ΛCDM being incomplete means it is wrong."** It fits every precision dataset with six parameters; incompleteness is measured as specific tensions, the normal state of a working model at the frontier.

## Quick Check

1. Why is the Bullet Cluster considered decisive evidence for dark matter over modified gravity?
2. What property must a cosmic component have to accelerate the expansion?
3. How did Type Ia supernovae reveal acceleration?
4. Why can't ordinary matter (baryons) be the dark matter?
5. Name two tensions or puzzles currently challenging ΛCDM.

**Answers:**
1. The lensing mass is spatially offset from the dominant baryonic component (the shock-lagged hot gas) — mass follows the collisionless component, which modified gravity tied to visible matter cannot reproduce.
2. Equation of state w = p/(ρc²) < −1/3, i.e. sufficiently negative pressure; Λ with w = −1 is the canonical case.
3. Distant (z ≈ 0.5) supernovae were ~25% fainter — farther — than a decelerating model predicts, meaning expansion sped up while their light travelled.
4. BBN and CMB independently fix baryons at ~5% of the critical density; dark matter is ~27%, collisionless, and needed before recombination when baryons were photon-coupled.
5. The Hubble tension (~73 local vs ~67.4 early), the S₈ structure-growth tension, small-scale core–cusp/missing-satellite issues, and the vacuum-energy magnitude problem.

## Takeaway

Cosmology's triumph is also its confession: a six-parameter model describes 13.8 billion years of history with percent-level precision, while naming 95% of its contents "dark". Dark matter built the cosmic scaffolding; dark energy now drives its expansion. Module 2 closes with the large-scale story told. Module 3 returns to the small end — neutron stars, black holes, and gravitational waves — where the strongest gravity in the Universe tests the same relativity that wrote the expansion equations.
