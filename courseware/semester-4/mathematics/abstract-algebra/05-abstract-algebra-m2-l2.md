# Cosets, Lagrange's Theorem and Index

## Overview
Cosets are the natural generalisation of multiples of $n$ in $\mathbb{Z}$. The cosets of a subgroup $H$ in $G$ partition $G$ into sets of size $|H|$, and the number of cosets is the *index* $[G : H]$. Lagrange's theorem — that $|H|$ divides $|G|$ — follows immediately. This lesson develops the coset machinery, proves Lagrange's theorem carefully, and surveys the subgroup correspondence for quotient groups.

## Learning Path
- What you should already know: the subgroup test, generators, the order of a group.
- What this lesson adds: cosets, index, a careful proof of Lagrange, the subgroup correspondence.
- What it unlocks: normal subgroups, quotient groups, and the isomorphism theorems (next lesson).

## Core Explanation
**Left cosets.** For a subgroup $H \subseteq G$ and $g \in G$, the *left coset* of $H$ by $g$ is $g H = \{g h : h \in H\}$. Cosets need not be subgroups (only if $g \in H$).

**Right cosets.** Similarly, $H g = \{h g : h \in H\}$. In non-abelian groups, left and right cosets differ.

**Equivalence relation.** Define $g_1 \sim g_2$ iff $g_1 g_2^{-1} \in H$. This is an equivalence relation:
- Reflexive: $g g^{-1} = e \in H$.
- Symmetric: if $g_1 g_2^{-1} \in H$, then $(g_1 g_2^{-1})^{-1} = g_2 g_1^{-1} \in H$.
- Transitive: if $g_1 g_2^{-1}, g_2 g_3^{-1} \in H$, then $(g_1 g_2^{-1})(g_2 g_3^{-1}) = g_1 g_3^{-1} \in H$.

The equivalence class of $g$ is $g H$ (left coset). So the left cosets partition $G$.

**Coset counting.** All cosets have the same size. The map $h \mapsto g h$ is a bijection $H \to g H$, so $|g H| = |H|$. Cosets partition $G$, so $|G| = (\text{number of cosets}) \cdot |H|$.

**Index.** The number of left cosets is $[G : H] = |G|/|H|$ (a positive integer). Sometimes written as $|G : H|$.

**Lagrange's theorem (proof).** Since the left cosets of $H$ partition $G$ and each has size $|H|$, the number of cosets is $|G|/|H|$, which must be an integer. So $|H|$ divides $|G|$. $\blacksquare$

**Subgroups of cyclic groups.** For $\mathbb{Z}_n$, the subgroups are in bijection with divisors of $n$. Each divisor $d$ of $n$ gives a subgroup of order $d$, generated by $n/d$.

**Index of a subgroup.** Always divides $|G|$. The index $2$ subgroups are always normal: if $[G : H] = 2$, there are only two cosets ($H$ and $gH$ for any $g \notin H$), and they must be equal to their right cosets.

**Cosets of $n\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.** The cosets are $0, 1, \ldots, n - 1$ (mod $n$). The index is $n$, consistent with Lagrange.

**Cosets of a finite-index subgroup.** If $H$ has index $n$ in $G$, the cosets give a map from $G$ to a set of $n$ elements. This is the beginning of a group action (the action on cosets by left multiplication).

**Action on cosets.** $G$ acts on the set of left cosets $G/H$ by $g \cdot (x H) = (g x) H$. The stabiliser of $x H$ is $x H x^{-1}$. The kernel is the *core* of $H$ in $G$, the largest normal subgroup contained in $H$.

**Quotient group.** If $H$ is normal in $G$ ($g H g^{-1} = H$ for all $g \in G$), then the cosets form a group $G/H$ under $(g_1 H)(g_2 H) = (g_1 g_2) H$. The identity is $H$; the inverse of $g H$ is $g^{-1} H$. The order is $|G|/|H|$.

**Examples of quotient groups.**
- $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}_n$. The subgroup $n\mathbb{Z}$ is normal (in the abelian $\mathbb{Z}$), and the cosets form $\mathbb{Z}_n$.
- $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is the unit circle (geometrically).
- $S_n / A_n \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ (the sign homomorphism).
- $\text{SO}(3)/\text{SO}(2) \cong S^2$ (the sphere — the space of rotations modulo rotations about a fixed axis).

**Subgroup correspondence.** Subgroups of $G/H$ are in bijection with subgroups of $G$ that contain $H$ (and are normal in $G$ if the subgroup of $G/H$ is normal). The "third isomorphism theorem" says $(G/H)/(K/H) \cong G/K$ for normal subgroups $H \subseteq K$.

**Second isomorphism theorem.** If $H \trianglelefteq G$ and $K$ is any subgroup, then $H K$ is a subgroup, $H \cap K \trianglelefteq K$, and $K/(H \cap K) \cong H K / H$.

**First isomorphism theorem.** If $\phi: G \to H$ is a homomorphism, then $\ker \phi$ is normal in $G$, and $G / \ker \phi \cong \text{im}(\phi)$.

**Examples of Lagrange's theorem.**
- A group of order $6$ has subgroups of order $1, 2, 3, 6$. Either $\mathbb{Z}_6$ (cyclic, with a subgroup of order $3$) or $S_3$ (no subgroup of order $6$ except the whole group, has three subgroups of order $2$).
- A group of prime order is cyclic (generated by any non-identity element), and is its own only non-trivial subgroup.
- $A_5$ has order $60$ but no subgroup of order $15$ (so not every divisor of $|G|$ is realised).

**Conjugacy classes and class equation.** A group acts on itself by conjugation. The orbits are the conjugacy classes, and the stabilisers are the centralisers. The orbit–stabiliser theorem gives $|C(g)| = |G|/|C_G(g)|$. The *class equation* is $|G| = |Z(G)| + \sum |C(g_i)|$ for a set of conjugacy-class representatives $g_i$ with $|C(g_i)| > 1$.

**Cauchy's theorem.** If a prime $p$ divides $|G|$, then $G$ has an element of order $p$ (and hence a subgroup of order $p$). Proof by induction on $|G|$.

**Sylow's theorems (preview).** If $p^k$ divides $|G|$, then $G$ has a subgroup of order $p^k$ (a *Sylow $p$-subgroup*). All Sylow $p$-subgroups are conjugate. The number $n_p$ of Sylow $p$-subgroups divides $|G|$ and $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod p$. These are deep results with many applications.

**Group extensions.** Given normal $H$ and quotient $G/H$, what are the possible $G$? This is the *extension problem*, generally hard. The split extension is the semi-direct product $H \rtimes G/H$.

## Key Ideas
- Left cosets $g H$ partition $G$.
- All cosets have size $|H|$; the number is $[G : H] = |G|/|H|$.
- Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$.
- Index-2 subgroups are normal.
- Quotient $G/H$ is a group iff $H$ is normal in $G$.

## Worked Examples
**Example 1 — Cosets of $3\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.** Cosets: $0 + 3\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots, -3, 0, 3, \ldots\}$, $1 + 3\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots, -2, 1, 4, \ldots\}$, $2 + 3\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots, -1, 2, 5, \ldots\}$. Three cosets, index $3$, consistent with $|3\mathbb{Z}| = \infty = |3\mathbb{Z}|$ and $|\mathbb{Z}| = \infty$. (Both infinite, but the index is $3$.)

**Example 2 — $S_3 / A_3$.** $A_3 = \{e, (1 2 3), (1 3 2)\}$ is the alternating group of order $3$. Cosets: $A_3$ (the even permutations) and $(1 2) A_3 = \{(1 2), (1 3), (2 3)\}$ (the odd permutations). Two cosets, index $2$, $S_3 / A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.

**Example 3 — Class equation of $D_6$.** $D_6$ has 6 rotations $\{e, r, r^2, r^3, r^4, r^5\}$ and 6 reflections. The centre is $Z(D_6) = \{e, r^3\}$, of order $2$ (the half-turn $r^3$ commutes with every element). Conjugacy classes: $\{e\}$, $\{r^3\}$ (the central classes), $\{r, r^5\}$, $\{r^2, r^4\}$, three reflections through vertices, three reflections through edges. Sizes: $1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3$. Class equation: $|D_6| = |Z(D_6)| + \sum |C(g_i)| = 2 + (2 + 2 + 3 + 3) = 12$. ✓

**Example 4 — Cauchy's theorem.** $|S_3| = 6 = 2 \cdot 3$. By Cauchy's theorem, $S_3$ has a subgroup of order $2$ (the transpositions) and a subgroup of order $3$ ($A_3$). ✓

## Common Misconceptions
- **"Cosets are subgroups."** They are not (unless the element is in the subgroup).
- **"Every subgroup is normal."** Only those with $g H g^{-1} = H$ for all $g$. In abelian groups, all subgroups are normal. In $S_3$, only $\{e\}$, $A_3$, and $S_3$ are normal.
- **"Every divisor of $|G|$ is a subgroup order."** No — $A_5$ has no subgroup of order $15$ despite $15 | 60$. Sylow's theorems give partial information.
- **"$G/H$ is always a group."** Only if $H$ is normal. Otherwise, the cosets still partition $G$, but the operation is not well-defined.

## Connections
Cosets are the foundation of quotient groups and the isomorphism theorems. Lagrange's theorem is the first major theorem of group theory; Sylow's theorems extend it. Quotient groups are the basis of Galois theory (where the field extensions correspond to subgroups of the Galois group), the classification of manifolds (via fundamental groups), and the symmetry breaking in particle physics.

## Takeaway
- Left cosets $g H$ partition $G$ into sets of size $|H|$.
- $[G : H] = |G|/|H|$, an integer (Lagrange).
- $|H|$ divides $|G|$; $|g|$ divides $|G|$.
- Index-2 subgroups are normal.
- $G/H$ is a group iff $H$ is normal.
