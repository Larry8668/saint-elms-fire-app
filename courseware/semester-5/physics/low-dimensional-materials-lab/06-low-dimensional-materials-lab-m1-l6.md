# Mini Project, Report Writing, and the Viva

## Overview

This lesson is the capstone of the Low-Dimensional Materials Lab. The student designs and executes a small project on a low-dimensional material: deposits a thin film, characterises it with multiple techniques, analyses the data, and writes a research-style report. The project should integrate the techniques from L1-L5.

The project is an opportunity to apply the characterisation techniques to a real material and to develop research skills: project planning, literature review, multi-technique characterisation, data analysis, report writing, and presentation.

## Learning Path

1. **Choose a project.** Possible projects include:
   - Deposit and characterise a metal film (e.g. Au, Ag, Al) by thermal evaporation.
   - Deposit and characterise a transparent conducting oxide (e.g. ITO, AZO) by sputtering.
   - Deposit and characterise a perovskite film (e.g. CH₃NH₃PbI₃) by spin coating.
   - Deposit and characterise a 2D material (e.g. MoS₂, graphene) by CVD or exfoliation.
   - Fabricate a thin-film transistor (TFT) and characterise it electrically.
2. **Plan the project.** Choose the material, the deposition method, the characterisation techniques. Write a project plan with a timeline.
3. **Deposit the film.** Follow the procedures from L1.
4. **Characterise the film.** Use at least three techniques from L2-L4 (e.g. UV-Vis, ellipsometry, Raman, AFM, SEM, electrical).
5. **Analyse the data.** Fit the models, extract the parameters, propagate the uncertainties.
6. **Write a report.** Use the research-style structure: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusions, references.
7. **Viva rehearsal.** Prepare for the viva; review the project, the characterisation, the analysis, and the conclusions.

## Core Explanation

### Project Design

A good project has the following characteristics:
- **Clear goal.** What is the scientific question? What property of the material is being measured?
- **Feasible scope.** The project can be completed in the available time with the available equipment.
- **Multi-technique characterisation.** At least three techniques are used to characterise the material. The results are cross-validated.
- **Comparison with literature.** The results are compared with the literature; the agreement or disagreement is discussed.

### Project Categories

#### Metal Thin Film

Deposit a metal film (e.g. Au, Ag, Al) on a glass or silicon substrate by thermal evaporation. Characterise by:
- UV-Vis: transmittance; compare with the literature.
- Ellipsometry: thickness and optical constants.
- Four-probe: sheet resistance; compare with the bulk resistivity.
- SEM: surface morphology; cross-section for thickness.
- AFM: surface roughness.

The project demonstrates the deposition and characterisation of a simple metal film.

#### Transparent Conducting Oxide

Deposit a TCO film (e.g. ITO, AZO) on a glass substrate by sputtering. Characterise by:
- UV-Vis: transmittance (should be > 80 % in the visible).
- Hall effect: carrier density and mobility.
- Four-probe: sheet resistance.
- XRD: crystal structure and grain size.
- AFM: surface roughness.

The project demonstrates the deposition and characterisation of a TCO, which is widely used in displays and solar cells.

#### Perovskite Film

Deposit a perovskite film (e.g. CH₃NH₃PbI₃) on a glass substrate by spin coating. Characterise by:
- UV-Vis: absorption edge; band gap (~ 1.55 eV).
- PL: emission peak (~ 770 nm).
- XRD: crystal structure; tetragonal or cubic phase.
- SEM: surface morphology; grain size.
- AFM: surface roughness.

The project demonstrates the deposition and characterisation of a perovskite, which is a promising material for next-generation solar cells.

#### 2D Material

Prepare a 2D material (e.g. MoS₂ monolayer by CVD or mechanical exfoliation) on a SiO₂/Si substrate. Characterise by:
- Raman: E¹₂g and A₁g peaks; separation ~ 19 cm⁻¹ for a monolayer.
- PL: emission peak (~ 660 nm for a monolayer).
- AFM: thickness (~ 0.65 nm for a monolayer).
- Optical microscopy: contrast on SiO₂/Si.
- FET: field-effect mobility.

The project demonstrates the preparation and characterisation of a 2D material.

#### Thin-Film Transistor

Fabricate a TFT with a thin-film channel (e.g. IGZO, MoS₂, a perovskite). Pattern the source, drain, and gate electrodes. Characterise by:
- Transfer characteristic: threshold voltage, field-effect mobility.
- Output characteristic: saturation current, on/off ratio.
- Stability: hysteresis, bias stress.

The project demonstrates the fabrication and characterisation of a TFT, which is the workhorse of display electronics.

### Project Plan

The project plan specifies:
- **Title and goal**: what the project is about.
- **Background**: a brief literature review (1 page).
- **Methods**: the deposition, the characterisation, the analysis.
- **Timeline**: a week-by-week schedule.
- **Expected results**: what you expect to find.
- **Risk assessment**: what could go wrong; what is the backup plan.

### Methods

The methods section describes:
- **Substrate preparation**: cleaning, drying, handling.
- **Deposition**: the source material, the chamber, the pressure, the deposition rate, the thickness.
- **Characterisation**: the techniques, the parameters, the data.
- **Analysis**: the models, the fits, the uncertainties.

### Results

The results section presents the data:
- **UV-Vis spectrum**: the transmittance and the Tauc plot; the band gap.
- **Ellipsometric data**: the thickness and the optical constants.
- **Raman spectrum**: the peaks and the interpretation.
- **AFM image**: the topography and the roughness.
- **SEM image**: the morphology and the cross-section.
- **Electrical data**: the I-V, the transfer characteristic, the mobility.

### Discussion

The discussion section interprets the results:
- **Comparison with literature**: do the results agree with the published values?
- **Cross-validation**: do the different techniques give consistent results?
- **Sources of error**: what are the dominant uncertainties?
- **Implications**: what do the results mean for the application?

### Conclusions

The conclusions section summarises the main findings in 1-2 paragraphs.

### Viva

The viva is a 15-30 minute oral examination. The examiner asks questions about:
- The project: what is the goal, what was done, what was found.
- The characterisation: why these techniques, what are the principles, what are the limitations.
- The analysis: what models were used, how were the fits done, what are the uncertainties.
- The literature: how do the results compare with the published values, what is the current state of the field.
- The implications: what do the results mean for the application, what would be the next step.

The student should be able to demonstrate the device (if applicable), answer the questions, and defend the conclusions.

## Key Ideas

- Multi-technique characterisation: at least three techniques, cross-validated.
- Project plan: goal, methods, timeline, expected results, risk assessment.
- Report: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusions, references.
- Viva: oral examination that tests the understanding of the project.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"The project is just a series of characterisations."** The project integrates the characterisations into a coherent scientific investigation. The cross-validation and the comparison with the literature are the key.
- **"The cross-validation is optional."** The cross-validation is the most important part of the project. It demonstrates that the results are reliable.
- **"The report is a record of what was done."** The report is a formal document that describes the question, the method, the results, and the conclusions. It should be self-contained and readable.
- **"The viva is a test of memory."** The viva is a test of understanding. The student should be able to explain the project in their own words.
- **"A small project is less valuable than a large project."** A small, well-executed project is more valuable than a large, poorly-executed project. The discipline of doing a small project well is the foundation of doing a large project well.
